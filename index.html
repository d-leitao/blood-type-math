How the score works

Pressure on a patient group is P = patients ÷ people who can donate to them. At 1.0 every compatible donor is needed for that group alone.

A donor’s score is θ + (1 − θ) × ΣP over the groups it can give to, where θ is the same-type share. Scores average exactly 1.0 across the population, so 2.0 means twice the average donor’s load. Counting reach alone would overrate common pairings: A+ patients can already take blood from most people.

Same-type first

The share of each group’s need met with the patient’s own type; the rest is shared across compatible donors. 0% is fully flexible, 100% gives every type exactly 1.0.

About 70% reproduces England’s O− figures: 7.8% of hospital patients, 13% of red-cell stock, and 41% of O− units given to patients who aren’t O− (NHSBT 2018 audit). No comparable data exists here for other countries or for plasma.

Assumptions

  • Patients and donors are spread like the population and everyone donates equally. Real patients skew older, and donor pools differ.
  • The shared part is spread evenly. In reality it’s mostly O− (emergencies, expiring units, special requirements), so only O−’s score is anchored to data.
  • Much plasma becomes medicines, where type doesn’t matter. The plasma view covers transfusion plasma only.
  • ABO and RhD only. Hospitals still crossmatch every unit, and other antigen systems matter for some patients.

Data
